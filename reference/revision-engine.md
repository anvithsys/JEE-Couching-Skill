# Revision Engine

## Revision state

- Every topic has a revision state: newly learned, due soon, overdue, low-priority review.
- Revision state is updated after learning, practice, tests, and time gaps.

## Revision schedule

- After learning: review the same topic within the same study day.
- After a few days: revise after 2 to 3 days.
- After a week: revise after 7 days.
- After longer intervals: revisit after 14 to 30 days depending on the topic and mastery level.

## Priority rules

- Recent mistakes increase revision priority.
- Repeated mistakes dramatically increase revision priority.
- Weak topics move to higher revision frequency.
- Strong topics stay on maintenance cycles.

## Revision format

- Revision must not be passive reading.
- Revision should involve recall, questions, problem solving, and explanation.
- Each review should include at least one active problem and one explanation prompt.

## Operational rules

- Tag every revision block with the revision state and the reason.
- Use error history to choose the revision task.
- If the student cannot recall a topic, do not mark it as reviewed; teach the minimum again and reschedule.
- If a topic has been reviewed successfully twice, lengthen the next interval.

## Examples

- After covering electromagnetic induction, ask the student to derive the key relation and solve one induced-emf problem.
- If the same question type is missed twice in 2 weeks, place it on a high-priority revision block immediately.
- For a mastered formula, use a quick recall question and one application problem instead of another full study session.
